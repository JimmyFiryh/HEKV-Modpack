Active Radar Homing Air-to-Air Missiles use the launching aircraft's radar for locking and guidance until the missile closes to its active radar range, at which point its own radar can guide it to the target without assistance from the launching aircraft.

ARH missiles behave and function identically to SARH missiles until they are within their active range. Once the missile goes active, the launching aircraft may maneuver freely without fear of losing lock. Active radar missiles are generally considered a direct upgrade over SARH missiles for this reason, at least in the realm of air-to-air weaponry. However, the AIM-54, being developed primarily to shoot down large targets at long range, does have worse overall performance than late generation SARH missiles within certain envelopes. F and M model Sparrows are generally a bette choice at closer ranges if the goal is to actually hit something.
